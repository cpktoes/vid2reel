<domain>
## Phase Boundary

`python -m v2r look` reads work/manifest.json (the working copies normalize made) and writes into work/: contact sheets (JPEG grids of stills, each labelled with its clip letter and time like "B 07.5") and work/look.json, which holds, for every half second of every clip, movement, camera motion and shake as distinct from subject movement, sharpness, brightness, sound loudness and shot changes; for every clip its overall exposure and colour balance; the list of every still with the sheet and cell it sits in; and a ranked list of up to five candidate moments per clip with start and end times and the reason in words. It measures and reports. It never chooses what goes in a reel.

Not in this phase: the cut list, `match` and the renderer (Stage 16); the handbook's choosing rules (Stage 17); sparser looking for long clips, time estimates and resumable runs (Stage 23); any change to the skill. EDITOR.md lines are proposed as text for the user to paste; the user writes EDITOR.md.

<decisions>
## Implementation Decisions

### The brief (locked by the user's prompt, from the guide's Stage 15, and the Decide box settled on 2026-10-17)
- **D-01:** A still is taken every second, plus extras at movement peaks and shot changes. This is the settled Decide box (CLAUDE.md "Settled so far", docs/decisions.md 2026-10-17); the rhythm is a setting.
- **D-02:** Contact sheets are grids of stills, each still labelled with its clip letter and time like "B 07.5" (seconds to one decimal, two digits before the point), large enough to read on their own. Sheet width, stills per sheet and the cap on sheets per run live in settings.py.
- **D-03:** work/look.json holds, for every half second of every clip: movement, camera shake as distinct from subject movement, sharpness, brightness, sound loudness and whether the shot changed; for every clip: overall exposure and colour balance (Stage 16's `match` reads these); and the ranked candidate moments with start, end and reason in words.
- **D-04:** The code measures and reports; it must not choose what goes in a reel. Ranking candidates is reporting facts in order; choosing is the chat's Claude's job, following the handbook. — **Reversibility:** one-way — the three-jobs rule in CLAUDE.md; an editing choice in look would have to be dug out of every later stage.
- **D-05:** Only FFmpeg features the sandbox's doctor report lists are used (Phase 2's D-06 carried forward); doctor grows for every new filter (see D-19).
- **D-06:** Input is the manifest with its frozen names (Phase 2's D-03). A skipped manifest entry gets a look.json entry marked skipped with its letter and no stills; letters are never renumbered.
- **D-07:** Tests use clips generated by FFmpeg with a known answer, parametrised over both shapes: a white square crossing the frame between seconds 4 and 5 must get a still that shows it; two patterns joined at second 3 must give a shot change and a still there; a jittering whole picture must read as camera shake with little subject movement, and a steady picture with a moving square the reverse; a blurred stretch must lower sharpness; two grey levels must order brightness; a beep against silence must show in loudness; a colour-cast clip must show in colour balance; the sheet cap and the extras cap must hold; a photo entry gets one still; a skipped entry is passed over.
- **D-08:** The run goes in the background by the handbook, not by the code (Phase 2's D-16 pattern): look prints one line per clip as it finishes (such as "B looked at in 21 s, 3 sheets so far") and rewrites look.json whole-or-nothing after every clip, the way normalize writes the manifest, so a run still going can be read and a stopped run loses only the clip it was on. A new look run replaces the last run's sheets and look.json.

### How it measures (the plan the user accepted on 2026-10-17; details are the planner's)
- **D-09:** One FFmpeg analysis pass per clip at a small frame size (about 320 pixels wide) and about 10 frames a second, using FFmpeg's built-in filters and nothing else: `deshake` (its `filename` option writes every frame's detected camera shift, angle and zoom to a text file; its steadied picture feeds the filters after it), `signalstats` (frame difference YDIF for movement, YAVG for brightness, the chroma, saturation and hue averages for colour balance), `blurdetect` (sharpness), `scdet` (shot-change score), with their per-frame values read back through the `metadata=print` filter; plus `ebur128` on the sound for loudness every tenth of a second. Values are summarised every half second (the planner picks mean or peak per measurement). Frame size, analysis rate and every threshold are settings. — **Reversibility:** reversible — any measurement can be swapped for another source later as long as look.json keeps its names.
- **D-10:** Camera motion versus subject movement: deshake's per-frame shift is the camera (a smooth shift is a pan, a jittery one is shake; both are reported, as motion magnitude and as a shake figure); frame difference measured on the steadied picture is subject movement. Known limit: deshake's block search saturates on a fast pan or a walking shot, so very large motion reads as "big camera motion" either way; the blind test judges whether the split is good enough to rank on. Fallback if it is not: report all movement and flag camera motion from the shift magnitude alone.
- **D-11:** No new Python library: Pillow (already a dependency) draws the sheets, and plain Python summarises a few hundred numbers per clip. NumPy, OpenCV and PySceneDetect were considered and not added (see D-18): every chat pip-installs requirements.txt before the first reel, so each dependency is paid for on every reel.
- **D-12:** Measured on the Mac held to two threads (which matched the sandbox in Stage 14): a 39-second 1080×1920 working copy took about 13 s for the analysis pass, under 1 s for loudness and about 9 s for 40 stills and their sheets; about 0.6 s of work per second of video. Decoding dominates, so the planner may share one decode between measuring and stills if it keeps the code plain.

### Sheets and stills (discussed)
- **D-13:** 12 stills per sheet: tall stills 4 across by 3 down, wide stills 3 across by 4 down. A still's orientation follows its working copy, so a sideways clip in a tall job gives wide stills. The sheet is sized so each still still reads after Claude's viewer shrinks the sheet (about 1,568 pixels on the long edge); the trial sheet of 1950 by 2586 pixels read clearly.
- **D-14:** A photo (a 4-second still clip from normalize) gets one still, labelled with its letter and 00.0.
- **D-15:** Short clips share a sheet: stills are laid out in letter order and fill sheets continuously, so a sheet may hold the end of one clip and the start of the next; the labels keep clips apart. Ten 5-second clips cost five sheets, not ten.
- **D-16:** The cap is 20 sheets per run (`MAX_SHEETS_PER_RUN`). Above it, every clip's base rhythm thins evenly (one still every 1.5 s, then every 2 s) while the extras are kept, look.json records that the rhythm was thinned and what it became, and look prints one plain sentence saying so.

### Extra stills (discussed)
- **D-17:** Extras are taken at peaks of subject movement (camera motion removed), at shot changes, and at loudness peaks (a laugh, a splash). An extra keeps at least 0.3 s from its nearest neighbour. A clip adds at most a third more than its base stills (a 30-second clip: 30 base stills and up to 10 extras), strongest peaks first. The spacing, the peak thresholds and the cap are settings.

### Candidate moments (discussed)
- **D-18:** Candidates are 2-second windows stepped by half a second; overlapping windows that score alike merge into one. Up to 5 per clip. Ranking: steady camera and sharp picture first, movement as the tiebreak; a window that is shaky or blurred never makes the list. Each reason names the facts and the stills inside the stretch, for example "steady camera, sharp, subject moving, sound peak at 04.5; stills A 04.0, A 04.5, A 05.0", so Claude's receipts are ready-made.

### Shot changes and libraries (discussed)
- **D-19:** Shot changes come from FFmpeg's built-in `scdet` filter. PySceneDetect (BSD-3-Clause) was checked: its default detector uses the same frame-difference method, but it needs OpenCV (an 80 MB download) or PyAV (43 MB) installed by pip in every chat. docs/licenses.md records that it was checked and why it was not pulled in; no library is added for this. — **Reversibility:** reversible — a settings switch and one function if the built-in filter misses cuts in real clips.
- **D-20:** doctor grows to list every filter look uses (`scdet`, `blurdetect`, `deshake`, `signalstats`, `ebur128`, `metadata`, `ametadata`, `fps`, `scale`, `select` or whatever the planner settles on), and the first task on branch `stage-15-look` is to run doctor from a phone ("use branch stage-15-look. Show me the doctor report in full") before anything depends on those filters. The sandbox's FFmpeg is 6.1; all of these exist there by version, but it is checked, not assumed.
- **D-21:** If a filter is missing where look runs, that measurement is marked unknown (null) in look.json for every half second, look prints one plain sentence naming the filter, and the sheets and the other measurements still come back. look never refuses a run for a missing measurement.
- **D-22:** Sheets are drawn with Pillow using its built-in scalable font (`ImageFont.load_default(size=...)`, Pillow 10.1 or newer; the project has 12.3). No font file is shipped or looked up on the computer.

### Claude's Discretion
- The look.json layout: small and plain, with its names documented in a docstring the way normalize documents the manifest, since Stages 16 and 17 read it. The per-clip progress line wording and the plain-English sentences.
- Sheet file names (for example work/sheets/sheet-01.jpg) and look.json's record of each still's sheet, cell and label; JPEG quality; the label box style (white text on a dark box in a corner was legible on the trial sheet).
- The exact analysis frame size, analysis rate, filter options, how each half-second summary is taken (mean or peak), how a peak is found (a local maximum above a threshold set from the clip's own spread, so a calm clip still yields its liveliest moments), and how the exposure and colour summary is expressed (for example mean luma, mean chroma offsets, mean saturation), as long as every number is a setting.
- Whether one decode is shared between measuring and stills, and whether stills are pulled by exact timestamps or by nearest analysed frame.
- What a photo gets as a candidate (none, or one entry marked as a photo).
- How doctor's lists grow without slowing doctor; how look checks them at start (Phase 2's `missing_ffmpeg_features` pattern).
- The additions to tests/media.py (a moving square by `overlay` with a time expression, jitter by `crop` with random offsets, a cut by concatenating two patterns, blur by `gblur` over a time range, loudness by the existing beep, a colour cast by `colorbalance` or a tinted `color` source).
- The EDITOR.md "look" step, proposed as text for the user to paste (the nohup pattern, reading look.log, showing the sheets to the user when asked), and the docs/decisions.md lines for the two places this differs from the guide (sound peaks as extras; FFmpeg's own filter rather than a separate library).

## Existing Code Insights

### Reusable Assets
- `normalize.write_manifest()` is the atomic-write pattern for look.json; `normalize.run_ffmpeg()` and `ffmpeg_timeout()` give each FFmpeg call time in proportion to the clip and clean up a half-written output.
- `normalize.clean_text()` and `display_name()` keep file names as data in sentences, the log and JSON.
- `doctor.ffmpeg_names("-filters")` and `missing_ffmpeg_features(filters=[...])` are the start-of-run check look needs for D-20 and D-21.
- `tests/media.make_clip()` already makes bars, marker and flat clips with or without sound, sideways, HDR-tagged; a moving-square pattern and a two-shot pattern are the main additions.

### Established Patterns
- One FFmpeg command per output, built as a Python list; errors are one plain-English sentence; the whole run never dies for one bad file.
- Nothing in tests/ assumes tall; every command takes the shape from the manifest (look needs no `--shape`: the manifest carries it).
- Pillow is imported lazily where it is needed, so video work keeps going if pip failed.
- The handbook, not the code, puts long commands in the background.

### Integration Points
- `v2r/__main__.py`: a `look` sub-command that reads `work/manifest.json` (an optional argument names another manifest or work folder for tests).
- `v2r/look/`: the package exists and is empty; the three jobs rule puts all measuring and sheet-making here.
- `v2r/settings.py`: sheet grid and width, stills per sheet, `MAX_SHEETS_PER_RUN`, base rhythm and its thinning steps, extras spacing and cap, peak thresholds, analysis frame width and rate, summary step (0.5 s), candidate window and step and count, JPEG quality, label font size.
- `v2r/doctor.py`: the longer `FILTERS` list.
- `docs/licenses.md`, `docs/decisions.md`, `EDITOR.md` (proposed text only), `README.md` (the look command).

### Measured facts (Mac, FFmpeg 8.0.1, two threads; the sandbox has FFmpeg 6.1, so re-check from a phone)
- `signalstats` exposes per frame `lavfi.signalstats.YDIF`, `YAVG`, `UAVG`, `VAVG`, `SATAVG`, `HUEAVG` and more; `scdet` exposes `lavfi.scd.score` and `lavfi.scd.mafd`; `blurdetect` exposes `lavfi.blur`; `ebur128=metadata=1` exposes `lavfi.r128.M` (momentary loudness) through `ametadata=mode=print`.
- `deshake=filename=PATH` writes a header line "Ori x, Avg x, Fin x, Ori y, Avg y, Fin y, Ori angle, Avg angle, Fin angle, Ori zoom, Avg zoom, Fin zoom" and one comma-separated line per frame; "Ori" is the detected motion.
- On a 39-second walking shot at 320 px wide and 10 fps: 386 frames measured; YDIF mean 17.7 and peak 29; scene score peak 7.4 (no cut; the filter's default threshold is 10); the analysis pass took 13.2 s, a plain decode alone 7.8 s, blurdetect as a separate pass 14.4 s, deshake as a separate pass 12.9 s, loudness 0.15 s, 40 labelled stills tiled into four sheets 8.8 s.
- Claude's viewer shrinks an image to about 1,568 pixels on its long edge; a 4-by-3 sheet of tall stills at 1950 by 2586 pixels still read clearly in a trial.

## Specific Ideas

- The user accepted the plan of 2026-10-17 as written: FFmpeg's own filters for every measurement, the deshake split for camera versus subject, Pillow for sheets, no new library, and the blind test as the judge of what the code cannot prove.
- The user's own test routine from the guide's Do-this: run look on a fixture set and open the sheets in Finder, asking "from this sheet alone, could I say what happens?"; the blind test after `/clear` with the guide's second prompt on five clips, one with something quick in it; each miss written in fixtures/reel-log.md, one sampling setting changed at a time until four of five picks match; then the phone test "use branch stage-15-look. Show me the contact sheets and tell me what happens in each clip", checking the sheets are readable on the phone and noting how long it took.
- Every change explained in plain English, with what to look at and where (which sheet, which label).
- Labels as in the guide: letter, space, seconds to one decimal ("B 07.5"); the label sits in a corner on a dark box.

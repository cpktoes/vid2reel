### Locked Decisions

**The brief (locked by the user's prompt, from the guide's Stage 15, and the Decide box settled on 2026-10-17)**
- **D-01:** A still is taken every second, plus extras at movement peaks and shot changes. This is the settled Decide box (CLAUDE.md "Settled so far", docs/decisions.md 2026-10-17); the rhythm is a setting.
- **D-02:** Contact sheets are grids of stills, each still labelled with its clip letter and time like "B 07.5" (seconds to one decimal, two digits before the point), large enough to read on their own. Sheet width, stills per sheet and the cap on sheets per run live in settings.py.
- **D-03:** work/look.json holds, for every half second of every clip: movement, camera shake as distinct from subject movement, sharpness, brightness, sound loudness and whether the shot changed; for every clip: overall exposure and colour balance (Stage 16's `match` reads these); and the ranked candidate moments with start, end and reason in words.
- **D-04:** The code measures and reports; it must not choose what goes in a reel. Ranking candidates is reporting facts in order; choosing is the chat's Claude's job, following the handbook. (One-way: the three-jobs rule in CLAUDE.md.)
- **D-05:** Only FFmpeg features the sandbox's doctor report lists are used (Phase 2's D-06 carried forward); doctor grows for every new filter (see D-19 [sic: D-20 below]).
- **D-06:** Input is the manifest with its frozen names (Phase 2's D-03). A skipped manifest entry gets a look.json entry marked skipped with its letter and no stills; letters are never renumbered.
- **D-07:** Tests use clips generated by FFmpeg with a known answer, parametrised over both shapes: a white square crossing the frame between seconds 4 and 5 must get a still that shows it; two patterns joined at second 3 must give a shot change and a still there; a jittering whole picture must read as camera shake with little subject movement, and a steady picture with a moving square the reverse; a blurred stretch must lower sharpness; two grey levels must order brightness; a beep against silence must show in loudness; a colour-cast clip must show in colour balance; the sheet cap and the extras cap must hold; a photo entry gets one still; a skipped entry is passed over.
- **D-08:** The run goes in the background by the handbook, not by the code (Phase 2's D-16 pattern): look prints one line per clip as it finishes (such as "B looked at in 21 s, 3 sheets so far") and rewrites look.json whole-or-nothing after every clip, the way normalize writes the manifest, so a run still going can be read and a stopped run loses only the clip it was on. A new look run replaces the last run's sheets and look.json.

**How it measures (the plan the user accepted on 2026-10-17; details are the planner's)**
- **D-09:** One FFmpeg analysis pass per clip at a small frame size (about 320 pixels wide) and about 10 frames a second, using FFmpeg's built-in filters and nothing else: `deshake` (its `filename` option writes every frame's detected camera shift, angle and zoom to a text file; its steadied picture feeds the filters after it), `signalstats` (frame difference YDIF for movement, YAVG for brightness, the chroma, saturation and hue averages for colour balance), `blurdetect` (sharpness), `scdet` (shot-change score), with their per-frame values read back through the `metadata=print` filter; plus `ebur128` on the sound for loudness every tenth of a second. Values are summarised every half second (the planner picks mean or peak per measurement). Frame size, analysis rate and every threshold are settings. (Reversible: any measurement can be swapped for another source later as long as look.json keeps its names.)
- **D-10:** Camera motion versus subject movement: deshake's per-frame shift is the camera (a smooth shift is a pan, a jittery one is shake; both are reported, as motion magnitude and as a shake figure); frame difference measured on the steadied picture is subject movement. Known limit: deshake's block search saturates on a fast pan or a walking shot, so very large motion reads as "big camera motion" either way; the blind test judges whether the split is good enough to rank on. Fallback if it is not: report all movement and flag camera motion from the shift magnitude alone.
- **D-11:** No new Python library: Pillow (already a dependency) draws the sheets, and plain Python summarises a few hundred numbers per clip. NumPy, OpenCV and PySceneDetect were considered and not added (see D-18 [sic: D-19]): every chat pip-installs requirements.txt before the first reel, so each dependency is paid for on every reel.
- **D-12:** Measured on the Mac held to two threads (which matched the sandbox in Stage 14): a 39-second 1080×1920 working copy took about 13 s for the analysis pass, under 1 s for loudness and about 9 s for 40 stills and their sheets; about 0.6 s of work per second of video. Decoding dominates, so the planner may share one decode between measuring and stills if it keeps the code plain.

**Sheets and stills (discussed)**
- **D-13:** 12 stills per sheet: tall stills 4 across by 3 down, wide stills 3 across by 4 down. A still's orientation follows its working copy, so a sideways clip in a tall job gives wide stills. The sheet is sized so each still still reads after Claude's viewer shrinks the sheet (about 1,568 pixels on the long edge); the trial sheet of 1950 by 2586 pixels read clearly.
- **D-14:** A photo (a 4-second still clip from normalize) gets one still, labelled with its letter and 00.0.
- **D-15:** Short clips share a sheet: stills are laid out in letter order and fill sheets continuously, so a sheet may hold the end of one clip and the start of the next; the labels keep clips apart. Ten 5-second clips cost five sheets, not ten.
- **D-16:** The cap is 20 sheets per run (`MAX_SHEETS_PER_RUN`). Above it, every clip's base rhythm thins evenly (one still every 1.5 s, then every 2 s) while the extras are kept, look.json records that the rhythm was thinned and what it became, and look prints one plain sentence saying so.

**Extra stills (discussed)**
- **D-17:** Extras are taken at peaks of subject movement (camera motion removed), at shot changes, and at loudness peaks (a laugh, a splash). An extra keeps at least 0.3 s from its nearest neighbour. A clip adds at most a third more than its base stills (a 30-second clip: 30 base stills and up to 10 extras), strongest peaks first. The spacing, the peak thresholds and the cap are settings.

**Candidate moments (discussed)**
- **D-18:** Candidates are 2-second windows stepped by half a second; overlapping windows that score alike merge into one. Up to 5 per clip. Ranking: steady camera and sharp picture first, movement as the tiebreak; a window that is shaky or blurred never makes the list. Each reason names the facts and the stills inside the stretch, for example "steady camera, sharp, subject moving, sound peak at 04.5; stills A 04.0, A 04.5, A 05.0", so Claude's receipts are ready-made.

**Shot changes and libraries (discussed)**
- **D-19:** Shot changes come from FFmpeg's built-in `scdet` filter. PySceneDetect (BSD-3-Clause) was checked: its default detector uses the same frame-difference method, but it needs OpenCV (an 80 MB download) or PyAV (43 MB) installed by pip in every chat. docs/licenses.md records that it was checked and why it was not pulled in; no library is added for this. (Reversible: a settings switch and one function if the built-in filter misses cuts in real clips.)
- **D-20:** doctor grows to list every filter look uses (`scdet`, `blurdetect`, `deshake`, `signalstats`, `ebur128`, `metadata`, `ametadata`, `fps`, `scale`, `select` or whatever the planner settles on), and the first task on branch `stage-15-look` is to run doctor from a phone ("use branch stage-15-look. - **D-21:** If a filter is missing where look runs, that measurement is marked unknown (null) in look.json for every half second, look prints one plain sentence naming the filter, and the sheets and the other measurements still come back. look never refuses a run for a missing measurement.
- **D-22:** Sheets are drawn with Pillow using its built-in scalable font (`ImageFont.load_default(size=...)`, Pillow 10.1 or newer; the project has 12.3). No font file is shipped or looked up on the computer.

## Summary

Everything D-09 asks for exists in FFmpeg 6.1 (the sandbox) with the same option names and metadata keys the Mac's 8.0.1 uses; I read the n6.1 source of each filter and ran the same chain on the Mac. One command per clip can read all the per-frame measurements: `fps=10,scale=320:-2,signalstats,blurdetect,scdet,deshake=filename=NAME,metadata=mode=print:file=NAME`. Loudness is a second, nearly free command on the sound alone. The numbers come back as plain text that a 20-line Python parser reads.

Two things the plan should know before writing tasks. First, stills cannot share the analysis decode: the extra stills are chosen from the measurements, so they are not known when the measuring command starts. The cheapest correct shape is two commands per clip: measure, then pull every still (base and extra) in one decode with a `select` expression and read them straight into Pillow through a pipe. Seeking to each still with `-ss` was about three times slower. Second, on real footage the camera/subject split in D-10 does not work by "measure frame difference on the steadied picture": deshake barely changes the picture, and on a real walking clip the frame difference was unrelated to deshake's shift (correlation 0.05). What does work is reporting three separate figures (all movement, camera shift, shake) and only calling movement "subject movement" in half-seconds where the camera is still. That is D-10's own fallback, and it uses the same filters.

**Primary recommendation:** Per clip, run (1) one analysis command with the chain above, (2) one loudness command, (3) one `select` stills command into a rawvideo pipe; summarise in plain Python with a centred moving average for shake and a median-plus-spread rule for peaks; draw sheets with Pillow at 1950 px wide, JPEG quality 80 to 85. Treat every threshold as a clip-relative rule, because real-footage values (blur especially) move only a few percent.

## 1. FFmpeg filters: exact options, keys and units

All facts below were read in the FFmpeg **n6.1 source** (`libavfilter/*.c` and `doc/filters.texi` from the n6.1 tag) and re-run on 8.0.1. Where 6.1 and 8.0.1 differ, it is said. Nothing below is newer than 6.1 unless marked.

### `deshake` (libavfilter/vf_deshake.c, n6.1)

Options [VERIFIED: vf_deshake.c:69-86]: `x`,`y`,`w`,`h` (search rectangle, default -1 = whole frame), `rx`,`ry` (search range in pixels, default 16, allowed 0 to 64), `edge` (blank/original/clamp/mirror, default mirror), `blocksize` (4 to 128, default 8), `contrast` (1 to 255, default 125), `search` (`exhaustive` default, or `less`), `filename`, `opencl` (ignored).

- **`rx` must be a multiple of 16** or the filter fails to start with "rx must be a multiple of 16" [VERIFIED: vf_deshake.c:352]. So `rx` is 16, 32, 48 or 64 (not 20 or 24). The docs do not say this.
- **What the search can see.** For each analysed frame it compares 16-wide blocks against the previous analysed frame within plus or minus `rx`/`ry` pixels (pixels of the analysed frame, i.e. at 320 px wide). A block whose best match is poor (sum of differences above 512) is thrown away [VERIFIED: vf_deshake.c:177]. The final shift is the most common block shift, clamped to plus or minus `2*rx`, `2*ry`; the angle is clamped to plus or minus 0.1 radian [VERIFIED: vf_deshake.c:309-311].
- **What happens beyond the range (saturation), tested.** A pan of 20 px per analysed frame (about 18 px at 320 wide, above the default 16) gave readings that fell back to 0 to 16 and jumped around, not a steady big number. A pan of 3.6 px per frame read 3.8, and 10.7 px per frame read 10.7 to 10.9 (accurate). So below about 12 px per analysed frame the shift is trustworthy; above the search range it is noise. On a real 39 s handheld clip only 6 of 386 frames sat at the 16 px limit; the median shift was 1.0 px, the 90th percentile 10.5 px.
- **`search=less`** tests every other position then the 8 neighbours instead of all positions [VERIFIED: vf_deshake.c:120-150 logic], about a quarter of the work per block. Not needed at 320 px; raising `rx` to 32 for fast pans costs about four times more per block.
- **`filename`** writes one header line and then one line per frame [VERIFIED: vf_deshake.c:357-361 for the header, :482 for the lines]. The header is exactly `Ori x, Avg x, Fin x, Ori y, Avg y, Fin y, Ori angle, Avg angle, Fin angle, Ori zoom, Avg zoom, Fin zoom`, and each line is 12 comma-separated `%f` numbers in that order.
  - **Ori** = the motion found between this frame and the previous one: x and y in pixels of the analysed frame, angle in radians, zoom in percent. This is the raw camera movement.
  - **Avg** = a one-sided exponential moving average of Ori with weight `2/20 = 0.1` per frame; the 20 is hard-coded (`refcount = 20`, vf_deshake.c:347), so the smoothing is about 2 seconds at 10 fps and cannot be changed.
  - **Fin** = the correction deshake applies, which is minus (Ori minus Avg) [VERIFIED: vf_deshake.c:462-478].
  - The **zoom columns were always 0** in every run, so ignore them. The first line is all zeros (no previous frame).
  - Row *i* of the text file is frame *i* of the filter's input; the file and the metadata file had the same number of rows in every run (386 and 386), so the two line up by index.
- **Finding: use Ori only, not Avg or Fin, and smooth it yourself.** Because Avg lags by about 2 s, the first 1 to 2 seconds of every pan have a large Fin, which reads as shake (tested: a clean 12 px/frame pan gave |Fin| of 6.1 in its first second, 2.3 in the next, 0.5 later). A centred moving average of Ori over about 1 s (10 frames) follows a pan with no lag and leaves the jitter. Shake = the length of (Ori minus that centred average); camera motion = the length of Ori. Tested on generated clips: steady pan (4 px/frame) gave camera 3.9, shake 0.65; random jitter gave camera 13, shake 12.6; a static picture with a moving square gave 0 and 0.
- **The steadied picture is of almost no use.** Deshake's output barely changes the frame difference (jitter: 17.9 before, 15.4 after; pan: 8.4 and 8.2; static: unchanged), because the correction it applies accumulates and decays. Nothing needs the steadied frames, so put `deshake` **last** in the chain, just before `metadata=print` (it only has to be in the chain so frames flow through it to write its file), and measure `signalstats`, `blurdetect` and `scdet` on the unsteadied picture (the bilinear resampling in deshake would soften the picture a little; for the tested clips the blur readings were the same either way).
- Cost: deshake is single-threaded; `-filter_threads` does not speed it up.

### `signalstats` (vf_signalstats.c, n6.1)

Per-frame metadata keys are all `lavfi.signalstats.<NAME>` [VERIFIED: vf_signalstats.c:719]. Seen in output (8.0.1; the same names are set by the 6.1 source): `YMIN YLOW YAVG YHIGH YMAX UMIN ULOW UAVG UHIGH UMAX VMIN VLOW VAVG VHIGH VMAX SATMIN SATLOW SATAVG SATHIGH SATMAX HUEMED HUEAVG YDIF UDIF VDIF YBITDEPTH UBITDEPTH VBITDEPTH`. Options (`stat`, `out`, `color`) are not needed.
- Y, U, V values are in code values 0 to 255 for 8-bit video [CITED: n6.1 doc/filters.texi, signalstats section]; the working copies are 8-bit, limited range (`YBITDEPTH=8` in every run), so black is about 16 and white about 235.
- **YDIF** = average absolute difference of all Y samples against the *previous frame this filter saw* (0 to 255; the first frame is 0) [VERIFIED: vf_signalstats.c:749 and doc]. At 10 fps that is the change over 0.1 s. Typical values measured: static picture 0; a small square crossing a bars picture 1.2 to 2.8; a whole-picture cut 123; a real handheld walking clip 14.6 (10th percentile) to 27 (90th).
- **UAVG, VAVG** are 128 for grey; their offset from 128 is the colour cast. A tinted clip read UAVG 120.4 and VAVG 137.0 against 128.7 and 128.0 for the untinted one. **SATAVG** is saturation (0 to about 181; 0 for a grey patch, 39 for the bars picture).
- **Do not use `HUEAVG` for colour balance.** It is an ordinary arithmetic mean of hue angles 0 to 360 [VERIFIED: vf_signalstats.c:701-707 sums `histhue[fil] * fil`], so reds that straddle 0 and 360 average to green. Use UAVG, VAVG and SATAVG (and YAVG for exposure).
- The 6.1 source also has a 16-bit path (line 975), so a 10-bit working copy would still work, but working copies are 8-bit.

### `blurdetect` (vf_blurdetect.c, n6.1; new in FFmpeg 5.1 per the n6.1 Changelog)

Options [VERIFIED: vf_blurdetect.c:72-78]: `high` (default 30/255), `low` (default 15/255), `radius` (1 to 100, default 50), `block_pct` (1 to 100, default 80), `block_width`, `block_height` (default -1 = whole picture), `planes` (default 1 = luma only). **The n6.1 documentation says the defaults are 20/255 and 50/255; the source and 8.0.1 say 15/255 and 30/255. Trust the source; do not set `low`/`high` at all.**
- Metadata key: `lavfi.blur` [VERIFIED: vf_blurdetect.c:316], printed with `%f`. It is the average width in pixels of the strong edges in the analysed frame (Marziliano blur metric), so **lower is sharper, higher is blurrier**, and the number depends on the analysed frame size and the picture. Sharp bars picture: 5.9; the same picture blurred (gblur sigma 10): 29 to 31.
- **A frame with no edges at all (a flat colour) gives `nan`.** Parse it as unknown (null), never as a number. Flat grey clips in the tests read `nan` for every frame.
- **On real footage the useful range is small.** On the real 39 s clip, frames that are visibly motion-blurred in the stills read 4.35 to 4.48; sharp ones read 3.95 to 4.23. That is only 10 to 15 percent. Measuring at 480 or 640 px wide stretched the numbers (4.2 to 5.2) but not the ratio. So: **no absolute "blurred" threshold and no fixed ratio like 1.5 times the median**; use the clip's own spread (median plus k times the median absolute deviation) and/or rank sharpness continuously. `radius` and `block_pct` made no real difference to the values; leave them at default.
- Cost: on the Mac's measurement in CONTEXT, `blurdetect` alone took about as long as the whole combined pass (14.4 s separate vs 13.2 s combined, including a 7.8 s decode), so it is the single most expensive measurement per frame. Running at 10 fps on 320 px keeps it affordable.

### `scdet` (vf_scdet.c, n6.1; present since FFmpeg 4.4/5.0)

Options [VERIFIED: vf_scdet.c:54-57]: `threshold` or `t` (0 to 100, default 10), `sc_pass` or `s` (default off).
- Keys [VERIFIED: vf_scdet.c:162-169]: `lavfi.scd.mafd` and `lavfi.scd.score` on **every** frame; `lavfi.scd.time` **only on frames at or above the threshold** (that is the cleanest "a cut happened" flag).
- The score is `min(mafd, |mafd - previous mafd|)`, clipped to 0 to 100 [VERIFIED: vf_scdet.c:131]. mafd is the mean absolute frame difference as a percentage of full scale, so a real cut is a spike in both. Tested: a hard cut gave 48.1; jitter gave up to 4.3; a blur switching on or off gave 3.6; a real walking clip peaked at 5.7 (the same order as CONTEXT's 7.4). The default threshold of 10 separated all of these.
- **Never set `sc_pass=1`**: it drops every frame that is not a cut, which would empty the rest of the chain. Leave it off.
- The flagged frame is the **first frame of the new shot** (its YDIF was 123, the previous frame's was 0).
- With `fps=10` before it, a cut is only seen to the nearest 0.1 s, and a dissolve (not a hard cut) will usually score below 10.

### `ebur128` with `metadata=1` (f_ebur128.c, n6.1)

Use it on the sound only: `-vn -af "ebur128=metadata=1,ametadata=mode=print:file=NAME" -f null -`.
- Options used: `metadata=1` [VERIFIED: f_ebur128.c:176]. Do not set `video`, `peak` (adds sample/true-peak keys you do not need).
- Keys [VERIFIED: f_ebur128.c:925-930], all prefixed `lavfi.r128.`: `M` (momentary loudness, 400 ms window, LUFS), `S` (short-term, 3 s), `I` (integrated), `LRA`, `LRA.low`, `LRA.high`; plus peak keys only if `peak=` is set. **Only `M` is needed for half-second loudness.**
- **Update rate: one set of keys every 100 ms** (`pts_time` 0, 0.1, 0.2 ...: 386 frames for 38.6 s) [VERIFIED by run; code comment "happens only once per filter_frame call", f_ebur128.c:900].
- **The value lags.** Tested with a 0.2 s beep at the start of each second: M stays at its floor until pts_time 0.3, reads -12.0 (full beep in the window) at 0.3, -15.0 at 0.4 and -51 at 0.5. The value printed with pts_time *t* describes the sound from about *t-0.3* to *t+0.1*, i.e. centred near *t-0.1*. So for the half-second starting at *a*, use the readings whose pts_time is from *a+0.1* up to (not including) *a+0.6*. Make the 0.1 a setting (for example `LOUDNESS_DELAY_SECONDS`). The first three readings (0 to 0.2 s) are always the floor.
- **Silence and edge values.** A track with no sound (what normalize adds) reads M = -120.691 constantly with I = -70.000 (8.0.1). In the beep test the value came back as `nan` in the quiet gaps after a loud moment (and a `-inf` is possible in 6.1: its source prints `%.3f` of a log of zero). So: **parse with `float()`, treat non-finite or below -70 as silent (null), never put `-inf` or `nan` in look.json** (JSON has no `nan`; Python's json would write the invalid word `NaN`). When the manifest says `sound_added` is true, skip this command and write null loudness.
- Cost: 0.15 to 0.2 s for a 39 s clip.

### `metadata` / `ametadata` print format (f_metadata.c, n6.1)

`metadata=mode=print:file=NAME` (video) and `ametadata=mode=print:file=NAME` (audio) write, for every frame that has any metadata:
```
frame:0    pts:0       pts_time:0
lavfi.signalstats.YMIN=0
...
lavfi.blur=5.891054
lavfi.scd.mafd=0.000
lavfi.scd.score=0.000
frame:1    pts:1       pts_time:0.1
```
[VERIFIED: f_metadata.c:338-340 for the format; seen in output]. One `frame:` header line then `key=value` lines, all keys of all filters upstream together, one block per frame. Parse with the regular expression `frame:(\d+)\s+pts:(-?\d+)\s+pts_time:(\S+)` and split other lines at the first `=`. Notes:
- **Without `file=` the text goes to the log at info level (stderr, hidden at `-loglevel error`).** Always give `file=`. [VERIFIED: f_metadata.c:264-282 and docs]
- **Each print line is cut at 127 bytes** (a 128-byte buffer, f_metadata.c:203); all the keys here are far shorter.
- The file is buffered; read it only after FFmpeg has exited.
- `pts_time` on the analysis frames is exact tenths (0, 0.1, 0.2 ...) because `fps=10` re-times the stream. Use `pts_time`, not the `frame:` counter, as the time.
- `file=` and `deshake`'s `filename=` sit inside the filter-graph string, where `:`, `,`, `'` and spaces in a path need escaping. **Run FFmpeg with `cwd` set to a work/temp folder and give both files plain names** such as `A.motion.txt` and `A.stats.txt`; never put a user's file name in a filter string. (The names come from the manifest letter, which is code-made.)

### Things not available in 6.1

Nothing used here is newer than 6.1. Avoid `-/filter_complex FILE` and `-/vf FILE` (FFmpeg 7.0); `-filter_script:v FILE` works in both. `-fps_mode passthrough` (FFmpeg 5.1) is already used in tests/media.py. [VERIFIED: tests/media.py:41 `"-fps_mode", "passthrough"`]

## 2. One decode, or two? Command shapes and cost

**Because extras are chosen from the measurements, the stills cannot be pulled in the same command as the analysis.** A `split` command *can* run both branches at once (tested; it works), but it can only extract stills whose times are known before it starts, i.e. the base rhythm. The extras would then need a second decode anyway. So the plain plan is **two decodes per clip: measure, then pull all stills at once.**

Measured on the Mac for the 38.6 s real clip at 1080×1920 (the machine was heavily loaded by other programs during the later runs, load average 14 to 74, so only the ratios are trustworthy; CONTEXT's figures stay the reference):

| Shape | What it does | Relative cost |
|-------|--------------|---------------|
| A. decode only | `-i clip -an -f null -` | 1 (CONTEXT: 7.8 s) |
| B. analysis pass | `fps,scale,signalstats,blurdetect,scdet,deshake,metadata` to null | about 1.2 to 1.7 (CONTEXT: 13 s) |
| C. all stills in one `select` | `select='eq(n,..)+..',scale` to a rawvideo pipe | about 1.1 to 1.5 (CONTEXT: about 9 s with sheets) |
| D. B and C in one command with `split` | works, but only for stills known in advance | saves about a third of B plus C, but extras still need another pass |
| E. one `-ss` command per still | one still per FFmpeg start | about 1.9 s each under load: 40 stills about 3 times slower than C |

Why E loses: working copies are made by libx264 with its default scene-cut keyframes, so keyframes sit 1 to 8 seconds apart (0, 2.2, 3.6, 4.7, 5.9, 7.8, 12.2 s ... in the real clip) and each `-ss` decodes from the keyframe before the time. So: **use shape C.** Expected total about 0.6 to 0.8 s of work per second of video on two processors (CONTEXT: 0.6), so a 30 s clip is about 20 s and the "two minutes per command" limit is not at risk for a normal clip; the handbook still starts it in the background (D-08).

**Command 1: measure (video).** Run with `cwd` = the work folder.
```bash
ffmpeg -hide_banner -loglevel error -nostdin -y -i A.mp4 -map 0:v:0 -an \
  -vf "fps=10,scale=320:-2,signalstats,blurdetect,scdet,deshake=filename=A.motion.txt,metadata=mode=print:file=A.stats.txt" \
  -f null -
```
Order matters: `fps` first (it throws frames away before any expensive work), then `scale`, then the three measuring filters, `deshake` last. Each filter you drop for D-21 is simply left out of the string; its keys are then absent and the parser returns null.

**Command 2: loudness (sound).**
```bash
ffmpeg -hide_banner -loglevel error -nostdin -y -i A.mp4 -map 0:a:0 -vn \
  -af "ebur128=metadata=1,ametadata=mode=print:file=A.loud.txt" -f null -
```

**Command 3: all stills for the clip.** `n` is the frame number, which equals `round(t*30)` because every working copy is constant 30 fps from 0 (Phase 2 guarantees this; `settings.OUTPUT_FPS` = 30).
```bash
ffmpeg -hide_banner -loglevel error -nostdin -i A.mp4 -an \
  -vf "select='eq(n,0)+eq(n,30)+eq(n,63)+...',scale=477:848:flags=area" \
  -fps_mode passthrough -f rawvideo -pix_fmt rgb24 -
```
- **`-fps_mode passthrough` is essential.** Without it the image writer repeats frames to keep a steady rate and you get hundreds of copies. [VERIFIED by run: with it, 12 requested gave exactly 12 frames, 40 requested gave 40.]
- Frames arrive in time order, each exactly `width*height*3` bytes; read them with `proc.stdout.read(n)` in a loop into `Image.frombytes("RGB", (w, h), data)`. No temporary image files and no second JPEG compression. (tests/media.py `gray_frame` already uses the raw-pipe pattern.)
- **Ask only for frame numbers that exist.** A last still requested past the end silently returns fewer frames; clamp each request to `round(seconds*30) - 1` using `working.seconds` from the manifest, and check that the number of frames read equals the number asked for.
- Expression length is no problem: 240 `eq(n,N)` terms is about 3 KB. For a very long list use `-filter_script:v FILE`.
- Cell size: scale straight to the final cell size in this command (shown: 477×848 for a 4-across tall sheet), so Pillow only pastes.

## 3. Contact sheets with Pillow 12

Tested on this Mac with a real 4 by 3 tall sheet; I opened the result and the labels and stills read clearly.

```python
# Source: Pillow 12.3 API, run on the Mac in this research
from PIL import Image, ImageDraw, ImageFont

SHEET_WIDTH, PAD = 1950, 8                      # settings
cols, rows = (4, 3) if tall else (3, 4)         # settings (stills per sheet = cols * rows = 12)
cell_w = (SHEET_WIDTH - (cols + 1) * PAD) // cols          # 477 for 4 across
cell_h = round(cell_w * (16 / 9 if tall else 9 / 16))      # or compute from working.size
sheet_h = PAD + rows * (cell_h + PAD)                      # tall: 1950 x 2576
font = ImageFont.load_default(size=34)          # built-in scalable font; no file needed (Pillow 10.1+)
sheet = Image.new("RGB", (SHEET_WIDTH, sheet_h), (24, 24, 24))
draw = ImageDraw.Draw(sheet)
# for each still: paste, then a dark box with white text in the top-left corner
x, y = PAD + (i % cols) * (cell_w + PAD), PAD + (i // cols) * (cell_h + PAD)
sheet.paste(still, (x, y))
label = f"{letter} {seconds:04.1f}"             # "B 07.5": two digits before the point, one after
left, top, right, bottom = draw.textbbox((0, 0), label, font=font)
draw.rectangle((x, y, x + (right - left) + 16, y + (bottom - top) + 10), fill=(0, 0, 0))
draw.text((x + 8 - left, y + 5 - top), label, font=font, fill=(255, 255, 255))
sheet.save(path, "JPEG", quality=85, optimize=True)
```
- `f"{7.5:04.1f}"` gives `07.5`, and `f"{0:04.1f}"` gives `00.0`, matching the label format. Clip times of 100 s or more print as `100.0` (the guide's width of two digits does not cover that; accept the longer label).
- **`ImageFont.load_default(size=...)` needs FreeType** (present in Pillow's pip wheels, including the Linux ones the sandbox installs; `features.check("freetype2")` is True here). Without FreeType, Pillow quietly returns a tiny fixed bitmap font and ignores `size`, so look should check `features.check("freetype2")` once and print a plain sentence if false rather than draw unreadable labels. [CITED: Pillow docs for `ImageFont.load_default`] `textbbox` returns the box including the font's own offset, which is why the code subtracts `left` and `top`.
- **Font size:** 34 px on a 477 px cell (about 7 percent of cell width) read clearly on the real sheet; scale it with cell width (a setting such as `LABEL_FONT_SHARE = 0.07`).
- **JPEG size** (real 4×3 tall sheet, 1950×2576): quality 70: 0.82 MB; 80: 1.00 MB; 85: 1.16 MB; 90: 1.42 MB. `optimize=True` saves 0 to 2 percent and costs up to 0.4 s; `progressive=True` saved another 4 percent. **Recommend quality 80 or 85**, with a cheap guard: if a saved sheet is over a setting `SHEET_MAX_BYTES` (1.5 MB), save again one quality step lower (for very noisy footage). Sheet saving takes 0.3 to 0.7 s.
- **Wide sheets:** 3 across at the same 1950 px width gives 638×359 cells and a sheet of about 1950×1500 px. Its long edge is the width, so after the viewer's shrink to about 1,568 px each still is about 510 px wide, which is more than a tall still gets (about 380×675 after the shrink of the 1950×2576 tall sheet). Both read.
- Claude's viewer shrinks to about 1,568 px on the long edge [CITED: CONTEXT.md D-13, from the trial]; the tall sheet is shrunk to about 1187×1568 (factor 0.61), so a 34 px label becomes about 21 px.
- **Mixed shapes on one sheet (open question below):** D-15 lets a sheet hold the end of one clip and the start of the next, but a sideways clip in a tall job makes wide stills, and the tall grid is 4 across while the wide grid is 3 across.

**Corrections the experiments forced:**
1. **Do not use `drawbox` for the moving square.** In `drawbox`, `t` in an expression means *thickness*, not time, so `x='if(between(t,4,5),...)'` never moved (the square never appeared). Use `overlay` with `eval=frame`, where `t` is time. [VERIFIED by run: drawbox version showed nothing at any time; overlay version showed the square 4.0 to 5.0.]
2. **Use a picture with 2D structure for any camera-motion test.** A vertical-bar picture has no detail up and down, so deshake's y readings are random; tests of jitter or pan should use `smptehdbars=size=1400x1000...,drawgrid=w=40:h=40:t=2:c=white@0.5,drawgrid=w=130:h=90:t=3:c=red@0.8` and crop from it. Keep pans slower than about 10 px per analysed frame (about 100 px/s at 360 wide); a 120 px/s pan over the repeating grid became unreliable later in the clip.
3. **A square that starts at x=0 exactly at second 4 already shows on the base still at 4.0**, and one at 5.0 vs -100 hides it. To prove the *extras* work (what LOOK-06 is for), make the event shorter than the one-second rhythm so that no base still can land on it: for example the square visible only between 4.2 and 4.8 (`between(t,4.2,4.8)`), and assert that a still exists in [4.2, 4.8] and that its cell contains the white square. A cut at 3.0 likewise coincides with the base still at 3.0; put the cut at 3.4 so the shot-change still is the one that matters.
4. **A big enough square.** A 40 px square on a 360×640 busy picture gave a bin mean YDIF of only 0.6, below a sensible floor; use a square about a fifth of the frame width (YDIF about 2 to 3).
5. **A blur that switches on or off also registers as "movement".** The blurred-stretch clip produced movement peaks at exactly 2.0 and 3.0 (YDIF 9.3), and a cut frame has a YDIF of 123. Tests that count extras must expect this, and the summaries should leave cut frames out of the movement average (pitfall 8 below).
6. **Photo test:** a 4 s still clip is `color=...` or `smptehdbars` with `duration=4`, analysed like any clip; but a photo entry should skip the analysis (one still at 00.0).
7. **How a test finds "the still that shows it".** The test can open the saved sheet with Pillow, crop the cell recorded in look.json, and check that a number of near-white pixels sit where the square must be (tests/media.py already has `corner_mean_image`-style helpers to extend).

Test speed: generating the clips takes about 0.3 s each; one `look` run on a 8 s 360×640 clip takes 1 to 3 s. Make the manifests by a tiny helper that writes `manifest.json` straight from generated clips (same names as normalize's docstring), rather than running normalize in every test.

## 5. Peak detection and ranking in plain Python

Prototype (run on the generated clips above; Python 3.9 on the Mac was enough for the prototype, the repo's 3.13 for real).

```python
import math, statistics

def finite(text):                        # "nan", "-inf", "" and missing all become None
    try:
        v = float(text)
    except (TypeError, ValueError):
        return None
    return v if math.isfinite(v) else None

def residual(series, width=10):          # shake: what is left after a centred moving average (a steady pan is taken out)
    half = width // 2
    return [series[i] - statistics.mean(series[max(0, i - half): i + half + 1]) for i in range(len(series))]
    # shake_i = math.hypot(residual(ori_x)[i], residual(ori_y)[i]);  camera_i = math.hypot(ori_x[i], ori_y[i])

def mad(values):
    m = statistics.median(values)
    return statistics.median(abs(v - m) for v in values)

def peaks(values, k, floor, min_gap):    # indexes of local maxima above median + k * max(MAD, floor), strongest first
    limit = statistics.median(values) + k * max(mad(values), floor)
    cand = [i for i, v in enumerate(values)
            if v >= limit and v >= (values[i - 1] if i else v) and v >= (values[i + 1] if i + 1 < len(values) else v)]
    cand.sort(key=lambda i: -values[i])
    chosen = []
    for i in cand:
        if all(abs(i - j) >= min_gap for j in chosen):
            chosen.append(i)
    return chosen
```
- **The floor on the spread is essential.** A calm clip has median 0 and MAD 0, so without a floor every tiny flicker is a "peak"; with `floor` about 0.25 to 0.5 YDIF units and `k` about 3 the generated square clip gave exactly one peak (in the 4.5 bin; the wide version in the 4.0 bin, both inside 4 to 5), the static clip none, and the cut clip one (at 3.0, the cut itself). These three numbers (k, floor, min gap) are settings; the blind test, not this research, sets their final values.
- Pick the peak's exact time from the **frame-level** series (the biggest frame inside the half-second), not the bin start, then convert to a still frame `n = round(t*30)`. For a cut, take the first frame of the new shot (`lavfi.scd.time` frame) plus about 0.1 s so the still is not the transition frame.
- **Peaks to look for, per D-17:** (a) movement, (b) frames with `lavfi.scd.time` (shot changes), (c) loudness (peaks of M, with a floor in LU, say 6 LU above the clip's median, converted back with the 0.1 s lag). Merge all of them, drop any within 0.3 s of a base still or another extra, keep the strongest first up to `len(base) // 3`.
- Where the camera is also moving, movement peaks come from the camera. On the real walking clip the correlation of YDIF with deshake's shift was 0.05 and YDIF stayed 11 to 29 whatever the shift; so **don't subtract a "camera part"**. Instead, report `movement` (all change), `camera` (px) and `shake` (px) separately, and call a half-second "subject moving" only where camera and shake are both below settings. Extras still use peaks of `movement` relative to the clip's own median (a jump over the clip's usual level is worth a still either way), but skip bins where `shake` is high (a bump is not an event).

**Candidates (D-18), tested.** Windows of 4 half-second bins stepped by one bin. Exclude a window if its mean shake is over a setting, if its blur is over the clip's median plus k spreads, or if a cut falls inside it. Tier 1 = camera still (mean camera under a setting), tier 2 = smooth pan; sort by tier then by movement; then merge. Three problems appeared that the planner must design for:
1. **Merging "alike" windows makes long stretches.** On a flat clip all windows score the same and merged into 3 stretches covering the whole 8 s; on the square clip into 3.0 to 6.0. Cap a merged stretch (for example 4 s, a setting) and stop merging when the stretch would pass the cap.
2. **A cut's YDIF (123) and a blur change's YDIF (9) inflate the window next to them.** The cut clip's best candidate was "3.0 to 5.0, movement 6.2", all of it the cut. Leave frames at or above the scdet threshold out of the movement, camera and shake means (and the frame just after a blur on/off if you want to be careful), and say "shot change at 03.0" in the reason instead.
3. **Pans rank above still shots by the movement tiebreak** (pan: movement 8.4). Keep the tier split (steady camera first) and say "camera panning" in the reason, not "subject moving".

For a clip with nothing moving the list should still give its sharpest steady stretches with the reason "steady camera, sharp, nothing moving" (LOOK-04 says up to five; it does not say a candidate must have movement).

Half-second bins: group frames by `floor(pts_time / 0.5 + 1e-9)` (five analysed frames per bin). Suggested summary per measurement: movement = mean YDIF; camera = mean of |Ori|; shake = mean residual length; sharpness = mean blur ignoring nan (null if all nan); brightness = mean YAVG; loudness = maximum M in the delayed window (null if silent); shot_change = true if any frame has `lavfi.scd.time`. Per clip: mean YAVG, mean UAVG-128, mean VAVG-128, mean SATAVG, plus YMIN/YMAX means for the exposure range.

### System Architecture Diagram

        │

   ┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
   │ photo? ──yes──► one still at 00.0 (select eq(n,0)) ──────────────┐   │
   │   │no                                                            │   │
   │   ▼                                                              │   │

   │     (skipped when working.sound_added)                           │   │

Plan the base rhythm first, then measure and extract.

### Recommended Project Structure
```
v2r/look/
├── __init__.py       # look(manifest_path) entry point, progress lines, look.json docstring (names frozen like the manifest's)
├── measure.py        # build the FFmpeg commands, run them, parse the text files (finite(), parse_stats, parse_motion, parse_loudness)
├── summarise.py      # half-second bins, peaks, shake residual, per-clip exposure and colour
├── pick.py           # base rhythm + extras, candidate windows and reasons
└── sheets.py         # extract stills (select + pipe), draw sheets with Pillow (imported lazily)
tests/test_look.py    # parametrised over both shapes
tests/media.py        # + patterns: square, cut, jitter, pan, blur, cast, one beep
```
(File split is a suggestion; `v2r/look/` currently holds only an empty `__init__.py`.)

### Pattern 1: whole-or-nothing look.json
Reuse `normalize.write_manifest`-style `.tmp` + `os.replace` (v2r/normalize.py:343-348 writes `manifest.json.tmp` then `os.replace`). Write after every clip; a stopped run leaves the last whole file.

### Pattern 2: missing filter means null, not refusal (D-21)
Build the filter chain from `{name: snippet}`: only include a snippet if `name in doctor.ffmpeg_names("-filters")`. `doctor.missing_ffmpeg_features(encoders=[], filters=NEEDED, decoders=[])` returns phrases but also runs three FFmpeg listings and returns `["FFmpeg"]` or `["ffprobe"]` if a tool is absent (v2r/doctor.py:72-95), so for per-measurement decisions call `ffmpeg_names("-filters")` once (v2r/doctor.py:66-69) and compare yourself. `fps`, `scale` and `metadata` are required for any measurement; if they are missing, all measurements are null but stills (which need `select` and `scale`) still come back; if `select` is also missing, that is the only case that cannot be served and needs its own sentence.

### Pattern 3: file names are data
Use `normalize.clean_text()` and `display_name()` for anything shown or stored from an original file name (v2r/normalize.py:70-91). Sheets and temp files use the letter only.

### Anti-Patterns to Avoid
- **Putting a path in a filter string.** Use `cwd` and plain names.
- **`-ss` per still.** About three times slower here.
- **`sc_pass=1`** (drops frames).
- **Absolute sharpness or hue thresholds** (real values move 10 to 15 percent; `HUEAVG` wraps).
- **Writing `NaN`/`-Infinity` into look.json** (invalid JSON for other readers); use `null`.
- **Letting look decide anything about the reel.** Reasons state facts ("steady camera, sharp, stills A 04.0..."), never "best" or "use this".

| Problem | Don't Build | Use Instead | Why |
|---------|-------------|-------------|-----|
| Shot-change detection | A frame-difference cutter | `scdet` (`lavfi.scd.time`) | Same method as PySceneDetect's default; already in FFmpeg |
| Camera motion | Optical flow in Python | `deshake` Ori columns | No NumPy; one text file |
| Sharpness | A Laplacian or FFT blur score | `blurdetect` | Built in; relative use only |
| Loudness | RMS of decoded samples | `ebur128` M | Perceptual weighting, 100 ms rate, 0.2 s per clip |
| Brightness and colour | Pixel loops in Pillow | `signalstats` YAVG, UAVG, VAVG, SATAVG | Free with the same pass |
| Still extraction | `-ss` loops or Python decoding | one `select` command to a raw pipe | Single decode, exact frames |
| Sheet text | A bundled font | `ImageFont.load_default(size=...)` | D-22 |
| Atomic JSON write | New temp-file code | the `write_manifest` pattern | Already tested in Phase 2 |

**Key insight:** every number in look.json is a mean, maximum or median of values FFmpeg already printed; the only real logic in Python is time-binning, a moving average and a spread-based peak rule.

## Common Pitfalls

### Pitfall 1: Reading Fin or Avg as shake
**What goes wrong:** Every pan starts with "shake" for 1 to 2 s. **Why:** deshake's average has a fixed 2 s lag. **Avoid:** use the Ori columns and a centred moving average. **Warning signs:** shake figures high at the start of every pan.

### Pitfall 2: Trusting the steadied picture
**What goes wrong:** the frame difference after `deshake` still contains the camera. **Why:** it barely changes the picture (17.9 to 15.4 on jitter; unchanged for pans), and on real walking footage YDIF and shift are unrelated (correlation 0.05). **Avoid:** report movement, camera and shake separately; say "subject moving" only where the camera is still.

### Pitfall 3: `nan` and `-inf`
`blurdetect` gives `nan` on a flat frame; `ebur128` gives `nan` or `-inf` after a loud moment into silence and about -120.7 for a silent track. Parse everything through `finite()` and map "silent" to null.

### Pitfall 4: Loudness is 0.1 to 0.3 s late
Window the readings as described (centre = pts_time - 0.1), or a beep at 04.0 will look as if it came at 04.3.

### Pitfall 5: Fast pans and walking shots
Above about 12 px per analysed frame (about 120 px/s on a 320 px frame) deshake's answer is noise. Flag a bin as "camera motion too large to measure" when the shift sits at the search limit (|Ori| at or near `rx`, `ry`) for many frames; D-10 already expects this. Raising `rx` to 32 doubles the range at about 4 times the search cost; not recommended by default.

### Pitfall 6: Pulling stills by frame number past the end
Clamp to the last frame and check the count read.

### Pitfall 7: Chains dropping frames or inflating readings
`fps=10` before `scale` and before everything heavy; never `sc_pass=1`; `deshake` last.

### Pitfall 8: Cuts and blur changes look like movement
Leave cut frames out of the movement, camera and shake means; mention the cut in words.

### Pitfall 9: Mixed shapes on a sheet
A sideways clip in a tall job is analysed fine (`scale=320:-2` follows the picture), but its stills are wide and do not fit the 4-across tall grid.

### Pitfall 10: Silent working copies
Normalize adds silence to clips with no sound (`working.sound_added` true). `ebur128` on it gives a floor value, not an error. Skip the loudness command and write null.

### Pitfall 11: `metadata=print` without `file=`
Goes to the log and is hidden at `-loglevel error`. Always give `file=`, in the work folder, then read it after FFmpeg exits.

### Pitfall 12: stderr from FFmpeg is the only error channel
Run with `-loglevel error`; keep the stderr tail with `normalize.tail`-style truncation if a command fails, and never let one failing measurement stop the clip's other measurements or the sheets (D-21).

## Code Examples

### Parse the measuring files
```python
import re

HEADER = re.compile(r"frame:(\d+)\s+pts:(-?\d+)\s+pts_time:(\S+)")

def parse_metadata(path):
    """List of {"t": seconds, key: text} dicts, one per frame block in a metadata=print / ametadata=print file."""
    frames, current = [], None
    for line in open(path, encoding="utf-8", errors="replace"):
        m = HEADER.match(line)
        if m:
            current = {"t": float(m.group(3))}
            frames.append(current)
        elif current is not None and "=" in line:
            key, value = line.rstrip("\n").split("=", 1)
            current[key] = value
    return frames

def parse_motion(path):
    """Rows of deshake's file: [ori_x, avg_x, fin_x, ori_y, avg_y, fin_y, ori_angle, ...]; the header line is skipped."""
    with open(path, encoding="utf-8") as handle:
        next(handle, None)
        return [[float(x) for x in line.split(",")] for line in handle if line.strip()]
```
(Frame *i* of the metadata file is row *i* of the motion file; if the counts differ, treat camera and shake as unknown for that clip.)

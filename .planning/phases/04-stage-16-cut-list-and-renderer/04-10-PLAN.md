1. docs/decisions.md: append these Stage 16 lines, dated 2026-10-19 for what the discuss step settled and with the date the task runs for what the build settled (the flagged assumptions of plans 01 to 09), each ending with how it differs from the guide, in plain words, naming no person or account:
   - The cut list is one JSON file per edit, `version` 1, every time in seconds on a working copy, each segment's start and length rounded to whole frames at 30 a second by one rule (nearest frame, half up). The guide's example has no version and no rounding rule.
   - A required `clips` block names each letter's original file and length, and check refuses a list whose letters do not match the work folder, so a list pasted into a new chat cannot cut the wrong clip. Not in the guide's example.
   - The canvas must match the shape the clips were normalized for: a wide version of a tall list means normalizing wide first, and check says so. The guide says only that the shape is one setting.
   - Each segment says its own `sound`, "keep" or "off", and must say it, instead of the guide's one top-level `"clips": "off"` switch.
   - A focus point has two parts, focus_x and focus_y: the spot in the picture that sits at the frame's centre when it can, ignored on an axis the clip already fills. The guide has focus_x only.
   - Every move is one amount, 4 percent of the frame (`MOVE_AMOUNT`), used in full at one steady speed; a pan zooms in by that 4 percent and travels exactly that far; moves are drawn with FFmpeg's perspective filter, which measured smooth where moving crops and the zoom-and-pan filter stuttered. The guide says "a few percent, with the amount capped".
   - The guide's "a photo gets a move by default" is the handbook's job: render never adds a move, check notes a photo with none, and every photo segment, moving or still, is built from the full-size upright copy normalize saved. The guide puts the default in the renderer.
   - The touch-up is written into the list by `match` as numbers per clip (brightness, blue and red in 8-bit code values, saturation as a multiplier), nudging each clip towards the median of the measured clips the segments use and never towards neutral grey, capped at ±8, ±4 and 0.92 to 1.08, every record rewritten on each run. The guide's example writes `"touch_up": "match"` and gives no numbers.
   - The loudness target is written in the list, by default -14 LUFS integrated with a -1 dBTP true-peak ceiling, measured with FFmpeg's ebur128 meter and applied as one plain gain, and render's last sentence states the figure. No platform publishes an official target; -14 is YouTube's and the common short-form figure. The guide asked to check the figure and report it.
   - The music drops by `music_under_sound` dB for the whole of every kept segment, with a 0.3-second ramp outside it; a track that runs out before the reel ends is refused; fades default to 0.5 s in and 1.5 s out; a reel with no sound at all is allowed with a note; a 220 Hz test tone ("test-tone") stands in until Stage 18, and no track has its own level before the final gain. The guide asks for music with a fade in and out.
   - The cover is named by segment and second and defaults to the reel's first frame. The guide asks for "a suggested cover still".
   - The proof sheet shows the first and last frame of every segment, labelled with its number and time on the reel, drawn with look's sheet code in work/proof/. The guide asks for a contact sheet of the finished video.
   - A long reel is fitted by join's own rule: past about 73 seconds the frame steps down to 2/3 and past about 146 seconds to 1/2, and render says so, so "at the canvas size" holds for shorter reels only. Requirement CUT-07 says "at the canvas size".
   - render builds each shot as its own scratch piece and encodes the reel once, because one FFmpeg graph for a whole reel used about 93 MB of memory per second of reel. Not in the guide.
   - check uses the jsonschema library (MIT) against schema/cutlist.schema.json; the shortest reel is 3 seconds, a list holds at most 120 segments, and a clip whose crop keeps less than half its picture gets a note. The guide asks for plain-English problems and gives no numbers.

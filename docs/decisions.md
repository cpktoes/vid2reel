# Decisions

One dated line per decision: the stage, what was decided, and how it differs from the guide (docs/field-guide.html). Where this file disagrees with the guide, this file wins. Never name a person or an account here.

- 2026-10-11 · Stage 16 · A wide clip in a tall reel is cropped to fill the frame, centred on the subject; never bars, never mixed with widescreen. Same as the guide.
- 2026-10-11 · Stage 19 · The chat asks a brief of three to five quick questions before editing. Same as the guide.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 10 · The toolbox reaches the phone through this public repo, fetched each time by a small skill. Same as the guide.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 23 · Long videos are finished on a Mac from start to finish. Same as the guide's Stage 23, but its Stage 12 still says only three Decide boxes are settled.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 09 · Python 3.13 is the minimum, matching the sandbox's Python 3.13.16. The guide names no version.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 11 · SKILL.md installs requirements with `pip install --break-system-packages`, which Linux sandboxes often require. Not in the guide's prompt.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 11 · The fit update caps the bitrate on the normal quality setting, and steps the frame down from full size to 2/3 and then 1/2 below 2.5, 1.2 and 0.7 Mbit/s (`FIT_STEPS` in settings.py). The guide gives the goal, not these numbers.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 11 · EDITOR.md starts the join in the background and reads join.log until it finishes, because the sandbox stops any single command after 2 minutes. The guide's five lines don't cover this.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 11 · EDITOR.md says one clip is fine to join, after the chat's Claude refused a one-clip join. The guide says only "join them".
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 11 · `MAX_ATTACHMENT_BYTES` is 400 MB, the app's warning point, with no margin below it, because a 408.7 MB file arrived. `MAX_ATTACHMENTS` is 10, the most files tried. The guide asked for limits with a safety margin.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 13 · The guide's sixteenth edition was made from Claude Code at my request: the published guide was edited directly and docs/field-guide.html replaced with the result. The guide's routine has the planning chat make revisions.
- 2026-10-12 · Stage 11 · The phone tests differed from the guide's table. Test 3 ran first with a 1-minute 1080p clip, then with a 30-second 4K clip. Test 4 used a 3-minute clip instead of 60 to 90 seconds. An extra test checked a 408 MB upload. Details are in docs/phone-limits.md.
- 2026-10-13 · Stage 14 · The working size is reel-sized: in a tall job, upright clips at most 1080×1920 and sideways clips at most 1920 pixels high; in a wide job, sideways clips at most 1920×1080 and upright clips at most 1920 pixels wide; nothing is ever enlarged (`WORKING_SIZE` in settings.py). Same as the guide's pick.
- 2026-10-13 · Stage 14 · Photos, including HEIC, are read with the Pillow and pillow-heif libraries, because the sandbox's FFmpeg 6.1 cannot open HEIC; FFmpeg only ever sees the upright JPEG copy normalize makes. The guide asks for FFmpeg features only.
- 2026-10-13 · Stage 14 · HDR clips are tone-mapped on the processor with FFmpeg's zscale and tonemap filters, the second-best method: the best one (libplacebo) needs a graphics driver the sandbox lacks, and the Dolby Vision layer iPhones add is ignored. The guide asked to be told.
- 2026-10-13 · Stage 14 · Normalize does not enforce `MAX_ATTACHMENTS`: it tries every attached file, and if a run fails as a whole its sentence asks for fewer clips. The guide refuses files by the Stage 11 limits.
- 2026-10-13 · Stage 14 · A file normalize cannot use keeps its letter and gets a "skipped" entry, so letters always match the order the files were attached. The guide does not say what happens to the letters.
- 2026-10-13 · Stage 14 · HDR clips are tone-mapped with hable at a peak of 203 nits, picked by eye from four-panel strips of the sample clips; research had found hable at 203 closest to Apple's own rendering. The guide names no tone-map method.
- 2026-10-17 · Stage 15 · Stills are taken one a second, plus extras wherever movement peaks or the scene changes, so a thirty-second clip is about forty stills on two or three sheets; the rhythm and the extras are settings in settings.py. Same as the guide's pick.
- 2026-10-17 · Stage 13 · The guide's seventeenth and eighteenth editions were made from Claude Code at my request, as the sixteenth was: the published guide was edited directly and docs/field-guide.html replaced with the result. The guide's routine has the planning chat make revisions.
- 2026-10-17 · Stage 15 · Extra stills are taken at sound peaks too (a laugh, a splash), as well as where movement peaks and where the shot changes; they share the extras' cap of a third more than the base stills. The guide names movement peaks and scene changes only.
- 2026-10-17 · Stage 15 · Shot changes come from FFmpeg's own scdet filter. The PySceneDetect library (BSD-3-Clause) was checked: its default method is the same frame difference, but it needs OpenCV or PyAV downloaded in every chat, so it was not added. The guide asked for a library check first, and this is its answer.
- 2026-10-17 · Stage 15 · Camera and subject: look.json reports all movement, the camera's movement and its shake as three separate figures, and calls a half second "subject moving" only where the camera is still, because on real footage the steadied picture barely changes how much of the frame differs. The guide asks for camera shake kept apart from subject movement; this split is trusted only while the camera is still.
- 2026-10-17 · Stage 15 · Short clips share a contact sheet: stills fill sheets in clip-letter order, so a sheet may hold the end of one clip and the start of the next, and a new sheet starts only when the stills change between tall and wide. The guide says short clips go on one sheet each.
- 2026-10-18 · Stage 15 · The cap is twenty sheets a run (`MAX_SHEETS_PER_RUN`), decided from the clips' lengths before anything is measured: above it every clip's base rhythm thins to one still every 1.5 s, then 2 s, while the extras are kept, and look.json says so. The guide asks only for a cap on sheets per reel.
- 2026-10-18 · Stage 15 · Candidate stretches are two seconds long, stepped by half a second, at most five per clip, ranked with a still camera first (a panning one below it) and movement as the tiebreak; a stretch with a shaky camera, a blurred picture or a cut inside it is never listed; each reason states facts and cites the stills it rests on. The guide asks for a ranked list of steady, sharp stretches with something moving and gives no numbers.
- 2026-10-18 · Stage 15 · The camera's own movement is measured with FFmpeg's deshake filter at a block-contrast threshold of 20 (`DESHAKE_CONTRAST`): at FFmpeg's default of 125 a pan on real phone footage read as a still camera, on the Mac and the phone alike. The guide names no method.
